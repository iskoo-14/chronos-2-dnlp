Interpretation of Feature Impact on Probabilistic Forecasting Performance
Cluster 0 — Small & Operationally Unstable Promotion-Driven Stores
For Cluster 0, the introduction of additional features leads to a substantial and consistent improvement in probabilistic accuracy. The mean ΔWQL of −0.0255 and median of −0.0239 indicate that, on average, stores in this cluster benefit noticeably from the enhanced feature set.
The relatively moderate standard deviation (0.0131) suggests that the improvement is broadly shared across stores, although some heterogeneity remains. The interquartile range (p25 = −0.0313, p75 = −0.0178) lies entirely below zero, confirming that the majority of stores experience a reduction in forecasting error. However, the presence of a small number of extreme negative outliers indicates that for certain shops, the added features enable particularly large performance gains.
Overall, these results suggest that for small and operationally unstable stores—where demand is strongly influenced by short-term shocks and irregular patterns—the inclusion of seasonal and correlation-based information provides valuable structure that Chronos is otherwise unable to infer reliably from the target series alone.

Cluster 2 — Stable Seasonal Stores
In contrast to the other regimes, Cluster 2 shows a much smaller and less consistent response to the inclusion of additional features. The mean ΔWQL of −0.0118 and median of −0.0061 indicate only marginal improvement, with several stores experiencing negligible gains or even mild degradation in probabilistic accuracy.
This interpretation is further supported by the large relative standard deviation (0.0199) and the upper quantiles: while the lower quartile (p25 = −0.0242) suggests that some stores do benefit, the 75th percentile is close to zero and the 90th percentile becomes positive, indicating that for a non-trivial fraction of stores the additional features provide no benefit or introduce noise.
These findings imply that Chronos already captures the dominant structure of stable, strongly seasonal demand regimes using the target history alone. As a result, additional seasonal and correlation-based covariates offer limited incremental information and may even disrupt well-learned patterns for certain stores.

Cluster 1 — Large Promotion-Driven Stores
Cluster 1 exhibits the largest average improvement among all demand regimes, with a mean ΔWQL of −0.0277 and a median of −0.0253. This indicates that large, promotion-driven stores benefit the most from the inclusion of additional features.
At the same time, this cluster displays the highest variability in performance gains, as reflected by a standard deviation of 0.0187. While the interquartile range (p25 = −0.0317, p75 = −0.0183) remains well below zero—demonstrating robust overall improvement—the wider spread and extreme negative outliers suggest that the magnitude of benefit varies considerably across individual stores.
This behavior is consistent with the heterogeneous nature of large promotion-driven stores: while some exhibit strong, feature-aligned seasonal and temporal patterns that Chronos can exploit effectively, others remain dominated by irregular promotional shocks. Consequently, the added features act as a powerful enabler for a subset of stores, resulting in both the highest gains and the largest dispersion in ΔWQL.
